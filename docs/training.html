What is being prepared?

This is data preparation for Humaneness Voice Small, a voice-generation model. It is separate from Humaneness Ears, the encoder used to estimate scores and locate vocal events. Preparing these examples does not report a new TTS training run or demonstrate a quality gain.

Instruction sourceRole

MOSS Audio Tokenizer v2Discrete audio targets; a different model from the two captioners

Existing, complete captions and acoustic predictions are reused. No new generative caption-model pass was needed. The original MP3s, annotation variants, generation prompts and exact voice IDs remain available. New local cue wording follows the frozen listening-demo format and retains all raw scores in the sidecar.

One clip, two representations

The full GENERAL/SCRIPT is the model instruction. The SCRIPT also occupies the native Text field. The frame budget is T, while the audio targets have shape [T,12]. The native processor creates 13 channels—one text/continuation channel and twelve codebooks—and masks instruction/reference/padding positions from loss.

PropertyValue and meaning
CodecMOSS Audio Tokenizer v2; pinned revision in the export lock
Timing48 kHz; one frame per 3,840 samples, or 80 ms
DepthFirst 12 RVQ codebooks, matching Humaneness Voice Small
StorageLittle-endian int16 NumPy [T,12]; indices 0–1023
Tail handlingExplicit zero-padding of less than one frame; exact true length saved for trimming decoded audio
ComputePersistent workers, four CPU threads/GPU, batch eight, 4.8-second streaming chunks, FP32 weights/quantizer and BF16 compute

Mono is duplicated into two identical channels for the stereo codec. No recording ends are dropped and no loudness normalization is added. Original MP3 bytes remain unchanged in the source archives; codec reconstruction is lossy. STOP and PAD are created by the training processor rather than stored among physical code indices.

Contents and verification

14,157,137 frames contain 169,885,644 codebook indices. The canonical captions retain 312,968 selected event intervals and 2,152,571 recognized words. The additive TAR payload is 6.33 GB; original audio is joined by ID and SHA-256 rather than duplicated.

The encoder validated shapes/ranges and audio durations. Each caption passed independent word/event/timing checks. A 128-clip pilot passed, including four codec reconstruction canaries. Native packing was checked with and without reference audio, confirming T+1 target steps and excluding reference STOP labels from supervision. One empty ASR output is explicitly marked, preserving provenance rather than replacing it with an intended script.

Full source coverage, unchanged source metadata and absence of scenario split leakage were audited before release. Mechanical validity does not imply correct emotions or event timing. The earlier 50-example audio-based automated review describes the format's perceptual limitations.
